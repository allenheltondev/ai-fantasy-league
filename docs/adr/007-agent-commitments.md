# ADR 007: Durable trade-interest commitments

Status: accepted, first slice of #215. The broader player-behavior roadmap is #219.

## Problem and decision

A chat reply can say "let me look at that" (#196), but the look was a fire-and-forget follow-up: a pitch that did not clear the agent's bar was skipped without a word, nothing remembered why, and nothing could come back to it when circumstances changed. Natural-language memory cannot say whether a promise was kept. Keep a small, typed commitment record beside the agenda (#214): what was pitched, where, which task owes the look, what it decided and why, and whether the one closing line went out.

The first kind is `trade_interest`: a person pitched a swap in chat, and the agent owes it a proper look. "I will evaluate" (`queued`/`evaluating`), "I sent it" (`waiting_for_partner`, with the real trade id), and the partner's answer are different states set only by deterministic transitions and operation results, never by a model's words.

## State and lifecycle

- Core owns the schema and pure transitions in `packages/core/src/agents/commitments.ts`: `openTradeInterest` (validation, idempotency per source message, duplicate detection, open-work limits), `advanceCommitment` (start, offered, closed, partner answer, redispatch), `expireCommitments`, `claimReply`/`settleReply`, and `dueCommitments`/`materialChange` for review.
- Repositories store one schema-versioned row per league, agent, and seat tenure: `pk=LEAGUE#<leagueId>`, `sk=AGENTCOMMIT#<agentId>#<occupiedSince-or-createdAt>`, revision-checked in DynamoDB with up to three compare-and-swap attempts, like the agenda. A new occupant starts with none. No migration or index is needed.
- A row holds at most three open commitments (one per counterpart) and twelve settled ones. A commitment records its source room, message id, and author (never the text), whether the room is a DM, the counterpart, validated player ids, the agenda goal the players could repair, the argument's verification (`pending`, `supported`, `unsupported`, `ignored_orders`, `unreadable`), its child task ids, the trade id, the latest decision with its structured reason and facts (score, bar, argument credit, lineup delta, the #216 attachment premium already included in the bar, open needs, received positions). A decline the attachment premium tips is recorded as `value_below_floor` (or `insufficient_depth` when the swap weakens the lineup): giving up that player is not worth it at the price, the reconsideration count, and the closing-line state. A swap the trade value math calls lopsided is declined as `lopsided`, whichever side it favours, never as `value_below_floor` (#219). A swap none of whose players has a projection yet, and that the value math cannot weigh either, is declined as `missing_data` ("I had no projections to value it by yet"). Neither is reconsidered. It expires four days after it was made, or at the trade deadline if sooner.
- Statuses: `queued`, `evaluating`, `waiting_for_partner`, `fulfilled`, `declined`, `cancelled`, `expired`, `failed`. A draft is validated before it is stored, so there is no persisted `proposed` state. Only the task currently assigned (the last child task id) may start or decide; a settled commitment accepts no second result.

## Runtime

1. **Intent.** A chat reply's trade takeaway still goes through #196's extraction and roster matching (`chatFollowUps`). With players both teams roster, `openInterest` records the commitment and hands `trade_proposal` a follow-up through #207's outbox, carrying the commitment's id and tenure. A new commitment takes one of the day's #196 chat-action uses before it is stored (the follow-up then does not take another); a redelivery or a duplicate takes none. With no room left (the open limit or the daily uses), the agent says so in the conversation instead of leaving a silent promise.
2. **Decision.** The follow-up (`beginLook`) skips when the seat changed hands (the old tenure's commitment is cancelled `seat_changed`, silently: the new occupant owes nothing), when another task owns it, when it is settled, or when its deadline passed (`expired`, with a line). Otherwise it re-reads the league: both teams must still roster every pitched player, trades must be open, and the swap goes through the unchanged #196 weighing (preview legality, lopsidedness, the archetype's bar, the difficulty's noise, `persuasionAllowance` only for an argument its own numbers support, nothing for orders).
3. **Result before words.** A decline is recorded with its reason and facts before its line; an offer only once `propose_trade` returned a trade id. The same conditional write persists a versioned delivery record: the already-redacted audience-specific text, stable per-look operation key, attempts, retry time, expiry, and an expiring owner lease. Posting uses that stable key across task ids. A crash before posting leaves an abandoned lease; a crash after posting but before acknowledgement safely replays the original operation. A failed send never borrows the model's line. Without a model (kill switch, spent budget), nothing is sent and the commitment ends `cancelled` (`autopilot`); a retry after a crash that sent the offer records the real offer from `list_trades` instead of sending another.
4. **Follow-through.** Each check-in first reviews commitments deterministically (`reviewCommitments`). A due closing reply becomes a `commitment_reply` follow-up through #207's existing task outbox; that delivery-only task never invokes a model or reruns the trade. Otherwise review reads what became of offers (`fulfilled`, partner declined/countered, withdrawn, expired, vetoed), expires looks never taken, resumes a queued look whose dispatch was lost for an hour, or reconsiders a decline. At most one follow-up per check-in, dispatched even when the check-in has nothing else to do (`TaskUnavailableError` may carry follow-ups). No new scheduler or polling loop is added.
5. **Reconsideration.** Only declines for `value_below_floor` or `insufficient_depth`, at most once, twelve hours after the decline and before the commitment's deadline, and only when the agenda now has an open starting slot the pitched players could fill that the decline did not record. It takes a chat-action use (none left: it waits for a later check-in). The new look re-validates everything and, if it clears, submits a fresh offer; in a DM its line recalls the earlier reason ("Earlier I passed because the value was not there for me. My RB situation changed, so I sent you an offer."). A partner's rejection or counter, a withdrawn, expired, or vetoed offer, and the other reasons are final: an old offer is never resurrected.

## Privacy and boundaries

Commitments never reach a prompt, natural-language memory, public APIs, or the activity log's text; the check-in's model is not told about them. Closing lines are deterministic except in a DM when the model's own reply fits the actual result. In a league room they never name players, values, or the agent's needs ("Took a proper look at that trade idea: check your offers."). In a DM they give a qualitative reason, never the agent's numbers or bar. The pre-existing `not_convinced` activity summary (with the swap and numbers) is unchanged.

## Guarantees and limitations

- Idempotency: the same message maps to one commitment; the same swap with the same partner to one open commitment; each follow-up has a task id from its trigger; the propose step replays by its idempotency key; the closing line is claimed once per look. Duplicate delivery or a crash cannot duplicate the offer or the closing line.
- A seat that stops being an agent's (a person takes it over) makes the runner skip the follow-up before the task runs (`no_agent_seat`), so its commitment stays `queued` in the old tenure's row. That is harmless: nothing reads that row again (every read is keyed by the current occupant's tenure, and a later agent occupant starts empty), no offer or line can come from it, and the person now managing the team owes nothing. An agent replacing an agent cancels it (`seat_changed`) on the next look.
- A transient refusal, including a chat rate or budget limit, is `withheld` until a bounded retry. Delivery uses a two-minute lease, five-minute retry delay, eight attempts, and a one-day window. Permanent room/access failures, league completion, and a changed seat tenure are terminal and recorded without exposing the message. Operators see state, attempt count, and reason, never the private text.
- Out of scope in this slice: lineup and waiver commitments, injury tips, the `trade_response` chat path (a push about an offer already made), and #211 evaluation. #218 can build conversational callbacks on the recorded reasons.

## Bursts of human messages

Added after the first slice. A person's message to an agent that the reply cooldown turns away is no longer dropped: the router defers one reply per agent, room, and person to when the cooldown ends, through the outbox and holding the chat slot for that time, and the rest of the burst joins it. A trade pitch inside a burst reaches this lifecycle through the one reply's takeaway, as before. A person may also keep talking to an agent without tagging it (conversation continuity); see the chat section of [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

- Every reply to a person coalesces (the router's immediate reply, a deferred one, and a check-in's hand-off): it answers the person's newest message to the agent in that room still unanswered, with up to five earlier unanswered ones listed by ref (`m1` oldest), asked to answer them all in one message. A message that lands before the reply is written joins it.
- Answered is explicit. A reply answers the message it replies to and the burst messages it names in `answersMessageIds`, which `post_message` checks (someone else's message in the same room, among its last 100, no newer than the one replied to) and stores on the reply. Nothing else counts: a newer message from the same person, the agent's unrelated line in a DM (an outreach, a closing line), or its reply to someone else never settles a question. Core `answeredBefore` is the one rule for the reply task, `burstOf`, and #218's `pendingQuestions`.
- The model may leave a burst message open (`leftOpen`, by ref) and say which message a takeaway comes from (`takeaway.ref`; the prompt says the newest terms win when an offer changed). Refs outside the supplied burst are ignored. A message left open, one that arrived while the model was writing, and one past the cap stay pending: the next reply or a check-in's hand-off answers it.
- Requests count without a question mark: `pendingQuestions` uses core `asksSomething`, a conservative rule (a `?`, or a sentence opening like a question or request: "what do you want for...", "lmk if..."). No extra model call.
- The reply claims each message it covers (`reply#<id>`) right before posting. A post that fails (the chat budget, a closed room) gives those claims back (`releaseOnce`), so the messages stay pending until a later reply or the question window's bounded expiry (a day).
- A pointer held for a person always has a scheduled reply behind it. When every reservation attempt loses the agent's chat slot, the router releases its own pointer (owner-checked), so the next message from that person schedules a reply, which lists the one that could not be deferred. Without a next message, a question among them is still handed on at the next check-in.
- The deferred reply is exactly one per burst while it waits. A redelivery that arrives after it ran may schedule another, which finds the burst answered and posts nothing (`already_answered`, no model call).
- Limits: whether the words actually answer a named message is the model's claim, checked only for refs; semantic quality is #247's evaluation. A correction across two separate replies opens a second look rather than replacing the first (each is bounded by the per-counterpart limit). Replies posted before `answersMessageIds` existed cover only what they reply to, so an earlier burst message from that time may be handed on once more within its day; its reply claim usually stops that.

## Validation

Core tests cover validation, idempotency, duplicate and limit outcomes, assigned-task-only transitions, partner answers, reconsideration cooldown/deadline/maximum, resume staleness and the task bound, expiry, bounded history, reply claims, and material-change detection. Both repository backends test tenure isolation and racing reply claims. A scripted multi-day scenario links a DM pitch, its commitment, a recorded decline, an injury, a reconsidered offer, and the partner's acceptance, with failure variants for orders in chat, a stale message, a gone player, a rejected offer never resurrected, duplicate delivery, a crash between offer and record, a lost dispatch, a seat change, expiry, the kill switch, a failed send, a model pass, a league room, no room for another look, and an unavailable store.
